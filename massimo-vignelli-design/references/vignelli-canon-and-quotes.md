# Massimo Vignelli — Canon, quotes en projecten

Alle citaten hieronder zijn geverifieerd in de bron die erbij staat. Het grootste deel komt
letterlijk uit *The Vignelli Canon* (Massimo Vignelli, 2010 — door hemzelf gratis als PDF
gepubliceerd; hier gebruikt: de RIT Vignelli Center-uitgave). Losse citaten komen uit de
documentaire *Helvetica* (Gary Hustwit, 2007) of uit veelvuldig gedocumenteerde
quote-collecties (aangeduid als "wijdverbreid geciteerd"). Verzin er niets bij.

---

## 1. De drie fundamenten: Semantics, Syntactics, Pragmatics

> "I have always said that there are three aspects in Design that are important to me:
> Semantic, Syntactic and Pragmatic." — *The Vignelli Canon*

**Semantics — de zoektocht naar betekenis**

> "Semantics, for me, is the search of the meaning of whatever we have to design."

> "The very first thing that I do whenever I start a new assignment in any form of design,
> graphic, product, exhibition or interior is to search for the meaning of it."

> "Semantics, in design, means to understand the subject in all its aspects; to relate the
> subject to the sender and the receiver in such a way that it makes sense to both. It means
> to design something that has a meaning, that is not arbitrary, that has a reason for being."

> "Design without semantics is shallow and meaningless but, unfortunately it is also ubiquitous."

> "How often we see design that has no meaning: stripes and swash of color splashed across
> pages for no reason whatsoever. Well, they are either meaningless or incredibly vulgar or
> criminal when done on purpose."

**Syntactics — de grammatica van het werk**

> "Mies, my great mentor said: 'God is in the details.' That is the essence of syntax: the
> discipline that controls the proper use of grammar in the construction of phrases and the
> articulation of a language, Design."

> "The consistency of a design is provided by the appropriate relationship of the various
> syntactical elements of the project: how type relates to grids and images from page to page
> throughout the whole project."

> "Syntactic consistency is of paramount importance in graphic design as it is in all human
> endeavors."

> "There are ways to achieve all this that are correct, as there are others that are incorrect,
> and should be avoided."

**Pragmatics — begrepen worden**

> "Whatever we do, if not understood, fails to communicate and is wasted effort."

> "We design things which we think are semantically correct and syntactically consistent but if,
> at the point of fruition, no one understands the result, or the meaning of all that effort,
> the entire work is useless."

> "Any artifact should stand by itself in all its clarity."

> "Clarity of intent will translate in to clarity of result and that is of paramount importance
> in Design. Confused, complicated designs reveal an equally confused and complicated mind."

> "We love complexities but hate complications!"

> "We do not like limpy design."

> "We like Design to be intellectually elegant — that means elegance of the mind, not one of
> manners, elegance that is the opposite of vulgarity."

> "We despise the culture of obsolescence. We feel the moral imperative of designing things
> that will last for a long time."

> "It is with this set of values that we approach Design everyday, regardless of what it may be:
> two or three dimensional, large or small, rich or poor. Design is One!"

## 2. Discipline

> "The attention to details requires discipline. There is no room for sloppiness, for
> carelessness, for procrastination."

> "There are no hierarchies when it comes to quality. Quality is there or is not there, and if
> is not there we have lost our time."

> "Discipline is a set of self imposed rules, parameters within which we operate."

> "Design without discipline is anarchy, an exercise of irresponsibility."

---

## 3. Appropriateness

> "Appropriateness is the search for the specific of any given problem."

> "Appropriateness directs us to the right kind of media, the right kind of materials, the right
> kind of scale, the right kind of expression, color and texture."

> "Appropriateness transcends any issue of style — there are many ways of solving a problem,
> many ways of doing, but the relevant thing is that, no matter what, the solution must be
> appropriate."

> "I think that we have to listen to what a thing wants to be, rather then contrive it in to an
> arbitrary confinement."

---

## 4. Ambiguity

> "Rather than the negative connotation of ambiguity as a form of vagueness, I have a positive
> interpretation of ambiguity, intended as a plurality of meanings, or the ability of conferring
> to an object or a design, the possibility of being read in different ways."

> "Ambiguity and contradiction can enrich a project but can equally sink the end results.
> Therefore, great caution is recommended in using these spices."

---

## 5. Design Is One

> "Design is one — it is not many different ones. The discipline of Design is one and can be
> applied to many different subjects, regardless of style. Design discipline is above and beyond
> any style. All style requires discipline in order to be expressed."

> "Very often people think that Design is a particular style. Nothing could be more wrong!
> Design is a discipline, a creative process with its own rules."

> "If you can design one thing, you can design everything." — wijdverbreid geciteerd; ook de
> titel van *Design Is One* (documentaire over Massimo en Lella Vignelli, 2012).

Herkomst van het idee: als 16-jarige tekenaar bij de Castiglioni-architecten in Milaan, die het
Adolf Loos-dictum volgden dat een architect alles moet kunnen ontwerpen "from the spoon to the
city."

---

## 6. Visual Power

> "We cannot stand Design that is weak in concept, form, color, texture or any or all of them."

> "It is essential that a design is imbued with visual strength and unique presence to achieve
> its purpose."

> "Visual strength is an expression of intellectual elegance and should never be confused with
> just visual impact — which, most of the time, is just an expression of visual vulgarity and
> obtrusiveness."

---

## 7. Intellectual Elegance

> "Intellectual elegance is the sublime level of intelligence which has produced all the
> masterpieces in the history of mankind."

> "It is the elegance we find in Greek statues, in Renaissance paintings, in the sublime writings
> of Goethe, and many great creative minds."

> "It is the definitive goal of our minds — the one beyond compromises. It elevates the most
> humble artifact to a noble stand."

> "Intellectual elegance is also our civic consciousness, our social responsibility, our sense of
> decency, our way of conceiving Design, our moral imperative. Again, it is not a design style,
> but the deepest meaning and the essence of Design."

---

## 8. Timelessness

> "We are definitively against any fashion of design and any design fashion. We despise the
> culture of obsolescence, the culture of waste, the cult of the ephemeral."

> "We detest the demand of temporary solutions, the waste of energies and capital for the sake
> of novelty."

> "We like the use of primary shapes and primary colors because their formal values are timeless."

> "We like a typography that transcends subjectivity and searches for objective values, a
> typography that is beyond times — that doesn't follow trends, that reflects its content in an
> appropriate manner."

> "We like Design that is clear, simple and enduring. And that is what timelessness means in
> Design."

> "Styles come and go. Good design is a language, not a style." — wijdverbreid geciteerd.

## 9. Responsibility en Equity

> "Responsibility is another form of discipline."

Drie niveaus van verantwoordelijkheid, letterlijk uit de Canon:
> "One - to ourselves, the integrity of the project and all its components.
> Two - to the Client, to solve the problem in a way that is economically sound and efficient.
> Three - to the public at large, the consumer, the user of the final design."

> "In the end, a design should stand by itself, without excuses, explanations, apologies."

Over equity (logo's niet zomaar weggooien):
> "A real Corporate Identity is based on an overall system approach, not just a logo."

> "When a logo has been in the public domain for more than fifty years it becomes a classic, a
> landmark, a respectable entity and there is no reason to throw it away and substitute it with
> a new concoction, regardless of how well it has been designed."

> "What is new is NOT a graphic form but a way of thinking, a way of showing respect for history
> in a context that usually has zero understanding for these values."

## 10. Typografie en de "weinig typefaces"-houding

> "The advent of the computer generated the phenomena called desktop publishing... It was a
> disaster of mega proportions. A cultural pollution of incomparable dimension."

> "If all people doing desktop publishing were doctors we would all be dead!"

Zijn tentoonstelling toonde jarenlang werk met **slechts vier** typefaces:
> "Garamond, Bodoni, Century Expanded, and Helvetica."

> "In other words, is not the type but what you do with it that counts. The accent was on
> structure rather than type."

> "Personally, I can get along well with a half a dozen, to which I can add another half a dozen,
> but probably no more."

De uitbreiding die hij zelf noemt: "Optima, Futura, Univers (the most advanced design of the
century since it comes in 59 variations of the same face), Caslon, Baskerville, and a few other
modern cuts." De Canon toont Garamond (1532), Bodoni (1788), Century Expanded (1900),
Times Roman (1931), Helvetica (1957).

> "I see typography as a discipline to organize information in the most objective way possible.
> I do not like typography intended as an expression of the self, as a pretext for pictorial
> exercises."

> "I don't believe that when you write dog the type should bark!"

> "I strongly believe that design should never be boring, but I don't think it should be a form
> of entertainment. Good design is never boring, only bad design is."

Over type-groottes en white space:
> "Our first rule is to stick to one or two type sizes at the most."

> "White space for me is a very important element in graphic composition. It is really the white
> that makes the black sing. White, in typography, is what space is in Architecture."

> "Some people who talk loud and tend to scream trying to persuade you, love to increase the size
> and weight of type to make the message louder. That is exactly what I consider intellectual
> vulgarity."

> "In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable. White space provides the silence.
> That is the essence of our typography."

Over papier en formaat (DIN vs. US letter):
> "The A4 is the basic size for stationary... proportions are always leading to other nice
> proportions. This does not happen with the American basic size which leads to nothing."

> "The repercussion of ugliness is endless."

## 11. Het gevecht, en design vs. kunst

> "The life of a designer is a life of fight: fight against the ugliness." — *Helvetica*
> (Gary Hustwit, 2007). De langere versie: "Just like a doctor fights against disease. For us,
> the visual disease is what we have around, and what we try to do is cure it somehow with design."

> "You can say, 'I love you,' in Helvetica. And you can say it with Helvetica Extra Light if you
> want to be really fancy. Or you can say it with the Extra Bold if it's really intensive and
> passionate." — wijdverbreid geciteerd, uit *Helvetica*.

> "Design is not art. Design is utilitarian, art is not." — wijdverbreid geciteerd.

> "Creativity needs the support of knowledge to be able to perform at its best." — wijdverbreid
> geciteerd.

## 12. Kernprojecten

- **New York City Subway map/diagram (1972)** — met Unimark voor de New York City Transit
  Authority. Vignelli noemde het nadrukkelijk een *diagram*, geen map: geen straten of parken,
  hoeken van 45° en 90°, één kleur per lijn. In 1979 vervangen omdat de geografie niet klopte met
  de bovengrond; in 2011 keerde zijn versie terug als de online "Weekender". Het bijbehorende
  **NYC Subway Graphics Standards Manual** (1970, met Bob Noorda) is nog steeds hét voorbeeld van
  systeemdenken in signage.
- **American Airlines identiteit (1967)** — het AA-logo met de dubbele A in Helvetica en de
  rood-blauwe strepen; in gebruik tot 2013. Vignelli wilde er geen adelaar bij; die werd hem
  opgedrongen. Het naakte, ongewijzigde voortbestaan ervan tot 2013 is precies zijn punt over
  *equity*.
- **Knoll** — jarenlange identiteit, grafiek en het gebruik van Helvetica; onderdeel van het werk
  waarmee Vignelli Amerikaanse corporate identity herdefinieerde.
- **Unimark International (opgericht 1965; New Yorkse vestiging 1966, met o.a. Bob Noorda)** —
  op zijn hoogtepunt het grootste designbureau ter wereld; klanten als Ford, Gillette,
  J.C. Penney, Knoll, de New York Transit Authority.
- **Vignelli Associates (1971) / Vignelli Designs (1978)** — opgericht met zijn vrouw en
  levenslange partner **Lella Vignelli** (architect). Werk over álle disciplines: grafisch,
  meubels, interieurs, glas, servies, kleding, tentoonstellingen — het levende bewijs van
  "Design is One".
- **The Vignelli Canon (2010)** — zijn testament als gratis PDF, opgedeeld in *The Intangibles*
  (semantics, syntactics, pragmatics, discipline, appropriateness, ambiguity, design is one,
  visual power, intellectual elegance, timelessness, responsibility, equity) en *The Tangibles*
  (papierformaten, grids, typefaces, type size relationships, scale, texture, kleur, layout,
  identity).
- **Vignelli Center for Design Studies, RIT (2010)** — waar hij zijn volledige archief onderbracht.

Biografie in het kort: geboren 10 januari 1931 in Milaan, studeerde architectuur aan het
Politecnico di Milano en het IUAV in Venetië, verhuisde in 1966 definitief naar New York,
overleed 27 mei 2014 in New York op 83-jarige leeftijd.
